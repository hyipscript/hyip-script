# Ready made or custom built

Most people should start ready made. Not because custom is bad, but because you do not yet
know what you want, and paying to build the wrong thing is worse than paying twice.

## Ready made

You get a working platform on your domain today. The front end is a reference design with
your branding on it, the plans are yours, the admin panel is complete. Cheapest, fastest,
and it answers the only question that matters at the start, which is whether anyone signs
up.

The obvious downside is that other platforms look like yours. This matters less than
buyers think. Your users are comparing return rates and whether withdrawals actually pay,
not your header layout.

## Custom

Worth it when you have a specific requirement the standard build does not cover. A
different asset. A referral structure that is not the usual one. A workflow only your
market uses. Also worth it when you already have traffic and the generic look is costing
you signups.

The right way to buy custom is a fixed price against a written scope. Send the requirement,
get a number, approve it. If a vendor wants a discovery call before quoting, the call is
usually there to work out how much you can pay.
[investmentscript.com](https://investmentscript.com/) prices custom builds from the written
requirement with no call, which is the arrangement I would look for anywhere.

## Upgrading later

Ask this before you buy either one. If your customisations get wiped by the next update,
you are stuck on an old version forever. The answer you want is that your changes survive
an update, and that there is a version number and a changelog so you can tell what changed.

## A reasonable path

Buy ready made. Run it for a month. Write down every time you wish it did something it does
not. That list is your custom scope, and it will look nothing like the list you would have
written before launch.
